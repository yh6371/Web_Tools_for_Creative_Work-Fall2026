# User Testing Reflection

## 1. Expectations vs. Actual Interactions

I expected users to treat Reference Playground as a living moodboard, where they could freely arrange images, explore unexpected relationships, and create connections between references.

During testing, most users found the concept interesting and the interactions intuitive. They were able to understand the main features without much explanation, which was encouraging.

However, I noticed that users wanted to interact with the references in more playful and dynamic ways. Rather than simply moving images and manually creating connections, they expected the references to respond to one another.

## 2. Feedback & Potential Improvements

Overall, the feedback was positive, especially regarding the concept, simplicity, and intuitive interface.

Some suggestions included:

- **Customizable connection colors:** Allow users to change the colors of lines to visually organize different relationships.
- **Physics-based interactions:** Introduce collision effects so references can bounce off or push each other.
- **Automatic connections:** Allow users to connect references by dragging them close together or overlapping them.
- **Canvas zoom:** Support zooming in and out to navigate larger collections of references.
- **More dynamic animations:** Explore additional movement behaviors to make the workspace feel more alive.

## 3. Reflection & Next Steps

The testing helped me realize that users were interested not only in organizing references but also in discovering relationships through interaction.

For the next iteration, I want to focus on making the connections between references feel more natural and playful, especially through proximity-based interactions and simple physics.

At the same time, I want to preserve the minimal interface and intuitive workflow. The goal is not necessarily to add more features, but to make the existing interactions feel more expressive and engaging.
