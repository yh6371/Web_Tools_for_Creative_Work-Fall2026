Raven Chen

Creative Process — UX / Interaction & Web-Prototype Projects

About the Process

Raven's creative process begins with real-world problems rather than technology. She defines a clear problem, sets boundaries early, and gradually moves from research and behavioral mapping to prototyping, testing, refinement, and documentation.

1. Identify the Core Tension

  • Raven rarely starts with a new technology or an idealized experience. Most projects begin with a real-world pain point or tension in an existing system.
  • She condenses the main conflict into one simple statement to keep the project focused.
  • She defines the boundaries of the prototype early, deciding what belongs in the current version and what should be saved for later.

2. Research & Context-Setting

  • She studies existing products and related projects to understand what has already been solved.
  • Research is not only for inspiration. She also considers practical limitations such as technology, time, and available resources.
  • Topics that are outside the scope of the current prototype are explicitly identified.

3. Map Human-System Behaviors

Raven focuses on interactions and behaviors before visual design.

  • User actions
  • Information received by the system
  • Feedback produced by the system

She uses notes and rough sketches to understand the entire interaction flow, including what information should be visible to the user and what should remain hidden.

4. Build a Minimal Working Prototype

  • She builds the smallest possible version that can test the core idea.
  • Interaction logic and functionality come before visual polish.
  • She uses AI-assisted coding tools, but defines the interaction logic, data fields, and constraints herself.
  • AI helps translate those requirements into code rather than making creative decisions.

5. Test the Prototype

  • Raven first tests the prototype herself and explores edge cases and possible failure scenarios.
  • A small group of participants then tests the prototype with minimal explanation.
  • She observes what people immediately understand, where they hesitate, and what unexpected behaviors appear.

6. Iterate & Simplify

  • The prototype is revised based on testing results.
  • Instead of endlessly fixing small problems, Raven often simplifies an idea if the original scope becomes too ambitious.
  • The project develops through repeated build → test → adjust cycles.

7. Refine the Full User Experience

Once the core interaction is stable, Raven begins refining the details of the experience.

  • Interface design
  • Hint and instructional text
  • Sound
  • Interaction feedback

The goal is to make these elements feel like parts of one cohesive system rather than separate effects.

8. Document Results & Limitations

  • She archives screenshots, recordings, diagrams, and process notes.
  • Limitations caused by technology, resources, or the nature of the prototype are documented clearly.
  • She separates issues that should be fixed now from ideas that belong in future versions.
  • She avoids presenting experimental prototypes as finished, production-ready products.

Process in Short

Real-world tension → Research & boundaries → Map human-system behaviors → Minimal prototype → Testing → Simplify & iterate → Refine experience → Document results & limitations

What stood out to me?

One thing I found interesting about Raven's process is how early she defines the limits of a project. Instead of trying to make a prototype solve everything, she treats reducing scope as an important part of the design process.
